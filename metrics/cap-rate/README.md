# Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)

Cap rate expresses a property's annual net operating income as a percentage of a stated property value or purchase price. It is an unlevered, single-period income yield.

## Formula

```text
Cap Rate = Annual NOI / Property Value
```

## Inputs and conventions

- NOI must exclude debt service, depreciation, income taxes, and owner-specific financing costs.
- Value may mean purchase price, current market value, or projected exit value; label it explicitly.
- Going-in cap rate normally uses stabilized or first-year NOI and acquisition price.
- Exit cap rate applies terminal-year NOI to an estimated disposition value, commonly using forward NOI.

## Interpretation

A lower cap rate usually means a higher price for each dollar of NOI; a higher cap rate may reflect higher income yield, risk, growth expectations, or property-specific issues. “Higher is better” is not universally correct because quality, location, durability of income, and capital needs differ.

## Worked example

With annual NOI of `$420,000` and a value of `$6,000,000`:

```text
$420,000 / $6,000,000 = 7.0%
```

## Limitations and checks

- Cap rate ignores leverage, future growth, capital expenditures, and the hold-period cash-flow path.
- Confirm that NOI and value use the same effective date and stabilization state.
- Review comparable-sales evidence, expense assumptions, lease rollover, deferred maintenance, and exit cap-rate sensitivity.
