# Cash-on-Cash Return (CoC)

Cash-on-cash return measures the annual pre-tax cash distributed to equity relative to the cash equity invested.

## Formula

```text
Cash-on-Cash Return = Annual Pre-Tax Cash Flow / Total Cash Invested
```

## Inputs and conventions

- Annual pre-tax cash flow is normally NOI less debt service and modeled recurring capital or reserve items.
- Total cash invested can include down payment, closing costs, initial repairs, financing fees, and other funded equity.
- State whether the numerator is year one, stabilized year, trailing period, or an average.
- Do not mix a partial-year numerator with a full investment denominator without annualization and disclosure.

## Interpretation

CoC is useful for current cash yield and liquidity planning. It does not capture appreciation, loan amortization, sale proceeds, or the timing of cash flows across the entire hold.

## Worked example

If invested cash is `$1,250,000` and annual pre-tax cash flow is `$100,000`:

```text
$100,000 / $1,250,000 = 8.0%
```

## Limitations and checks

- Leverage can raise CoC while also increasing default and refinancing risk.
- Deferred capital spending can temporarily inflate the result.
- Compare CoC with DSCR, debt yield, IRR, equity multiple, and reserve adequacy.
