# Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM)

GRM relates property price to gross annual rental income. It is a quick valuation screen, not a full underwriting measure.

## Formula

```text
GRM = Property Price / Gross Annual Rental Income
```

## Inputs and conventions

- State whether rent is actual, trailing, current scheduled, or market rent.
- Use annual rent before operating expenses.
- Treat ancillary income consistently across properties.
- Price and rent should reflect the same property and effective period.

## Interpretation

A GRM of `8.0x` means the price equals eight years of the stated gross rent before expenses. Lower GRM may suggest a lower price per dollar of rent, but expense burden, vacancy, condition, and growth may explain the difference.

## Worked example

For a `$4,800,000` property with `$600,000` in annual gross rent:

```text
$4,800,000 / $600,000 = 8.0x
```

## Limitations and checks

- GRM ignores vacancy, operating expenses, capital expenditures, financing, and income quality.
- It should be compared only among similar assets using consistent rent definitions.
- Use NOI, cap rate, comparable sales, and a full cash-flow model before making a decision.
