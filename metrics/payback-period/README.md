# Payback Period

Payback period measures the time required for cumulative net cash distributions to recover the initial invested capital.

## Formula

For level annual cash flow, a simplified estimate is:

```text
Payback Period = Initial Investment / Annual Net Cash Inflow
```

For uneven cash flows, accumulate each period's distribution until cumulative inflows equal the invested capital; interpolate within the recovery period when appropriate.

## Inputs and conventions

- Include the same initial equity components used in the investment analysis.
- Define whether sale proceeds count toward recovery.
- Use net distributions after the modeled operating, financing, and capital items.

## Interpretation

A shorter payback period indicates faster capital recovery and generally lower exposure duration. It does not measure profit earned after recovery or the time value of money.

## Worked example

An investment of `$600,000` generating `$120,000` per year has a simplified payback period of:

```text
$600,000 / $120,000 = 5 years
```

## Limitations and checks

- The simplified calculation is unreliable for uneven cash flows.
- Payback ignores cash flows after recovery and does not discount future money.
- Review IRR, equity multiple, absolute profit, liquidity, and downside scenarios alongside it.
