# Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI is a simple cumulative return measure comparing net profit with invested capital.

## Formula

```text
ROI = Net Profit / Total Investment
Net Profit = Total Proceeds - Total Investment
```

## Inputs and conventions

- Total investment should include the equity and costs included in the analysis scope.
- Total proceeds may include operating distributions and net sale proceeds.
- State the measurement period and whether the result is cumulative or annualized.

## Interpretation

A `25%` cumulative ROI means profit equals 25% of the defined investment basis. ROI is easy to understand but does not account for when cash flows occur; the same ROI over two years and ten years represents very different performance.

## Worked example

If total invested capital is `$800,000` and total proceeds are `$1,000,000`:

```text
($1,000,000 - $800,000) / $800,000 = 25.0%
```

## Limitations and checks

- ROI definitions vary, so confirm the numerator, denominator, and period.
- It can omit opportunity cost and reinvestment timing.
- Review IRR, equity multiple, annual cash flow, hold period, and downside cases with ROI.
